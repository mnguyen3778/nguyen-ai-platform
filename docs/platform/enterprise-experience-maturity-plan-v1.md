# Enterprise Experience Maturity Plan v1

## 1. Constitutional Purpose

Enterprise Experience Maturity Plan v1 defines the Phase III maturity roadmap
for externally consumed enterprise experiences in the Nguyen AI Platform.

This document is a constitutional planning artifact only. It exists to mature
how governed platform outputs are experienced by client organizations,
executive stakeholders, and business users without changing approved
architecture, governance, repository ownership, capability ownership, or
contracts.

The purpose of this plan is to improve:

- Commercial Readiness
- Capability Cohesion
- Strategic Alignment
- Explainability
- Traceability
- Repository Independence
- Architectural Simplicity

## 2. Bounded Responsibility

This plan owns one bounded constitutional responsibility:

- enterprise planning for externally consumed platform experience maturity

This plan does not own:

- implementation architecture
- UI design
- UX design
- page layouts
- APIs
- AWS services
- runtime behavior
- repositories
- production code
- data models
- deployment
- contracts
- technology selections
- product functionality
- implementation sequencing

The plan evaluates and guides experience maturity. It does not design or
implement experiences.

## 3. Enterprise Scope

The scope of Enterprise Experience Maturity Plan v1 is the maturity of
externally consumed enterprise experiences, including:

- Commercial Engagement
- Business Readiness Assessment Experience
- Executive Experience
- Executive Reporting Experience
- Portfolio Intelligence Experience
- Evidence Intelligence Experience
- Strategy Engagement Experience
- Client Delivery Experience
- Case Study Experience

These are experience domains over approved platform capabilities. This plan
does not redefine existing capabilities.

## 4. Constitutional Context

The Nguyen AI Platform is an enterprise Evidence Intelligence Platform. Its
mission is to transform governed evidence into trusted executive intelligence.

Externally consumed enterprise experiences must present governed evidence,
assessment truth, executive intelligence, reporting, portfolio context, and
client engagement without moving producer responsibilities into presentation
capabilities.

This plan derives from and remains consistent with:

- Foundation Baseline Review.
- Phase I - Platform Architecture Foundation.
- Phase II - Platform Governance Foundation.
- Platform Governance Phase II Completion Review.
- Phase III - Platform Evolution Planning.
- Platform Capability Architecture Baseline v1.
- Platform Capability Maturity Review v1.
- Executive Intelligence Product Architecture v1.
- Evidence and Lineage Maturity Plan v1.

This plan does not supersede or amend any approved constitutional baseline.

## 5. Planning Objectives

Enterprise Experience Maturity Plan v1 has the following planning objectives:

- mature externally consumed experiences as a coherent enterprise experience
  layer
- preserve the Website as presentation consumer only
- preserve Website Projection Delivery Contract boundaries
- improve commercial clarity without introducing marketing content
- improve executive readability without defining UI or UX design
- preserve evidence lineage and explainability across presentation
  experiences
- prevent presentation-side interpretation from becoming executive
  intelligence generation
- prepare the platform for future cross-repository readiness review

## 6. Maturity Dimensions

Experience maturity is evaluated through the following enterprise dimensions:

- Business Alignment: the experience supports the evidence-to-intelligence
  mission.
- Capability Cohesion: the experience aligns to approved platform
  capabilities without duplicating them.
- Producer/Consumer Preservation: the experience preserves upstream producer
  authority and downstream consumer boundaries.
- Evidence and Lineage Preservation: the experience keeps governed outputs
  traceable and explainable.
- Contract Boundary Preservation: the experience relies on approved
  architecture contract boundaries where governed outputs are consumed.
- Commercial Readiness: the experience can support client understanding and
  executive decision-making at planning level.
- Governance Alignment: the experience remains consistent with approved
  governance baselines.
- Repository Independence: the experience does not couple repositories or move
  responsibilities.
- Planning Completeness: the experience is sufficiently mature for future
  constitutional planning.

## 7. Maturity Stages

The maturity stages for externally consumed enterprise experiences are:

- Defined: the experience domain is identified, bounded, and mapped to
  approved platform capabilities.
- Governed: the experience domain preserves repository ownership,
  producer/consumer boundaries, evidence lineage, explainability, and
  applicable contract boundaries.
- Enterprise Ready: the experience domain is mature enough to support future
  cross-repository readiness review and commercial planning without requiring
  architecture redesign, governance redesign, or repository ownership change.

Maturity stages are planning-level only. They do not indicate implementation
completeness, production readiness, operational readiness, UI completion, or
runtime quality.

## 8. Planning Principles

Enterprise experience maturity is governed by these planning principles:

- Experiences present governed outputs; they do not create truth.
- Experiences preserve producer-owned meaning.
- Experiences must remain traceable to approved upstream evidence and
  executive intelligence outputs.
- Experiences must improve commercial clarity without weakening governance.
- Experiences must not introduce implementation design.
- Experiences must not redefine platform capabilities.
- Experiences must not move ownership into the Website for convenience.
- Experiences must preserve the Website Projection Delivery Contract boundary
  where executive intelligence is presented.
- Experiences must preserve human decision authority.
- Experience maturity must reduce future constitutional growth, not increase
  it unnecessarily.

## 11. Repository Boundaries

This plan preserves approved repository boundaries.

Platform Repository:

- owns constitutional architecture, governance, capability architecture,
  platform evolution planning, architecture conformance, cross-repository
  coordination, and release governance
- does not own implementation, runtime behavior, assessment truth, executive
  intelligence production, or website presentation

Assessment Service:

- remains sole producer of deterministic assessment truth
- remains owner of Business Decision Package production
- does not own executive intelligence or presentation experiences

Executive Intelligence Platform:

- remains sole producer of executive intelligence
- remains producer of Website Projection Delivery Contracts
- does not own assessment truth or presentation experiences

Website:

- remains presentation and customer experience owner
- consumes Website Projection Delivery Contracts only when presenting
  executive intelligence
- does not derive assessment truth, perform business decisions, or generate
  executive intelligence

## 13. Producer / Consumer Boundaries

Enterprise experience maturity must preserve the approved producer and
consumer model:

```text
Assessment Service
        |
        v
Executive Intelligence Platform
        |
        v
Website
        |
        v
External consumers
```

Producer rules:

- Assessment Service remains the sole producer of assessment truth.
- Assessment Service remains the producer of Business Decision Packages.
- Executive Intelligence Platform remains the sole producer of executive
  intelligence.
- Executive Intelligence Platform remains the producer of Website Projection
  Delivery Contracts.
- Website produces presentation only.
- Platform Repository produces constitutional architecture and governance
  artifacts only.

Consumer rules:

- Website consumes Website Projection Delivery Contracts only where executive
  intelligence is presented.
- External consumers receive presentation experiences, not producer authority.
- Presentation capabilities preserve producer-owned meaning.
- Consumers must not become co-producers through presentation or engagement.
